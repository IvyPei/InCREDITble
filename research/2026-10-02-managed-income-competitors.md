# InCreditble: managed bond income and cash-account competitors

Research date/access date: **2026-10-02**. Scope: US retail alternatives to the current amount → risk → periodic income → portfolio → spending-account prototype. Official provider documentation is used below; promotional yield comparisons are not treated as independent evidence. This is product research, not investment advice.

## Finding

**The broad product combination already exists.** Wealthfront is the most direct managed substitute examined here: personalized bond ETFs, automatic dividend transfers to its Cash Account, and debit-card spending. Fidelity offers another strong substitute by combining bond tools, income distributions, and cash management. Betterment directly overlaps with the risk/income choice, although its ordinary nonretirement withdrawal automation has documented limitations.

The opportunity therefore needs to be a specific unmet job—such as explaining and managing a reliable spending budget from a particular customer's assets—not merely simpler bond selection or putting interest on a debit card. That is analysis, not a proven market conclusion.

### Wealthfront: closest integrated comparison

- Its bond portfolio uses ETFs and personalizes allocation using tax filing status, household income, and state. Automatic rebalancing and tax-loss harvesting are included. The official support page was updated **September 6, 2026**. Its SEC yield is an annualized recent income measure, not a forecast of total return. [Automated Bond Portfolios support](https://support.wealthfront.com/hc/en-us/articles/14756022051988-Automated-Bond-Portfolios)
- The product launch article, with financial figures updated September 8, 2026, states a **$500** minimum and distinguishes ETF expenses from its **0.25%** advisory fee. [Product launch / minimum](https://www.wealthfront.com/blog/introducing-automated-bond-portfolio/)
- Crucially, the **January 14, 2026** product update confirms dividend sweeping for its Automated Bond Portfolio into the Cash Account. Do not rely on older copy saying all dividends must be reinvested. [Dividend sweeping announcement](https://www.wealthfront.com/blog/what-we-shipped-h2-2025/)
- ETF dividends generally arrive around the start of a month, with a December/January exception; this is not equal weekly income. Withdrawals involving ETF sales generally take 3–4 business days. [Bond portfolio product page](https://www.wealthfront.com/automated-bond-portfolio)
- Its separate individual-Treasury ladder charges **0.15%**, starts at **$500**, permits **$100** additions, and offers ladder lengths from **6 months to 6 years**. Turning reinvestment off sends matured principal plus interest monthly to Cash. Selling early can lose principal; withdrawals typically take 2–3 business days. The official help page (July 1, 2026) still contains inconsistent target-date wording (“in the near future” followed by an explanation), so target-date availability should be reconfirmed in account before asserting universal availability. [Ladder support](https://support.wealthfront.com/hc/en-us/articles/23265024478484-Automated-Bond-Ladders)
- Cash Account offers spending/checking functionality including an optional debit card. Checking features have a separate partner-bank identity-verification requirement. [Cash Account](https://www.wealthfront.com/cash)

**Analysis:** “Tax-aware bonds → cash → card” cannot carry differentiation on its own. InCreditble's direct corporate holdings and explicit spending schedule may differ, but their net benefit versus these inexpensive ETF/Treasury alternatives must be demonstrated.

### Betterment: risk/income selection is already a product

- **June 25, 2026** legal disclosure describes the new Target Income built with BlackRock strategy: primarily bond ETFs, no stock exposure, and **four** profiles—Core, Moderate, High, Aggressive Income. These target yield **ranges**, not a fixed yield percentage. BlackRock provides allocations; Betterment executes with discretion. This strategy lacks Betterment tax-loss harvesting and auto-adjust/glide-path features. [Current strategy disclosure](https://www.betterment.com/legal/income-portfolio-strategy)
- Digital fees: **$5/month** below **$24,000** household eligible balance without at least **$200/month** recurring deposits; otherwise **0.25% annually**. ETF expense ratios are additional. At a stable $10,000 balance, $5/month is $60/year or 0.60%, before fund expenses—an arithmetic illustration, not the rate for every account. [Fees, June 18, 2026](https://www.betterment.com/help/fees)
- No maintained investment-account minimum; **$10 ACH deposit minimum**. Disclosure currently states **$50** managed-portfolio minimum for rebalancing, subject to portfolio/price variation. [Portfolio minimum disclosure](https://www.betterment.com/legal/portfolio-minimum)
- Official help states ordinary nonretirement accounts do **not** support recurring withdrawals; the feature is for retirement-income goals and has conditions including dismantling tax coordination. [Recurring withdrawals](https://www.betterment.com/help/recurring-withdrawals-retirement-accounts)
- Betterment has Checking with no account fee or required minimum and Cash Reserve without a fee charged on the balance. These do not establish an automatic Target Income → weekly spending pipeline. [Checking minimums](https://www.betterment.com/help/checking-account-fees-minimums), [Cash Reserve fees](https://www.betterment.com/help/cash-account-fees), [Checking/debit overview](https://www.betterment.com/help/tag/checking-account)

**Analysis:** The slider itself is easy to copy and already has a risk-profile analogue. A possible gap is understandable, flexible nonretirement spending automation; verify this with real user tasks before claiming a competitive advantage. Old Betterment pages mentioning five profiles refer to legacy material; this report uses the June 2026 disclosure's four.

### Fidelity: the default “already have an account” alternative

- Bond Ladder Tool builds staggered individual holdings and charts estimated principal and income. Depending on the selected bonds, income can be monthly, quarterly, or semiannual. [Bond Ladder Tool](https://www.fidelity.com/fixed-income-bonds/fixed-income-tools-services/bond-ladder-tool)
- Individual bond minimums generally range **$1,000–$5,000**; a diversified portfolio needs multiple bonds. Online secondary Treasury purchases are free; other secondary bonds cost **$1 per bond**. This is the stated commission/markup, not proof of zero spread or market impact. [Income-products comparison](https://www.fidelity.com/fixed-income-bonds/compare-income-products)
- Auto Roll reinvests principal for eligible auction Treasuries/new-issue CDs, not arbitrary corporate bonds or all secondary holdings. Interest is not included in subsequent roll purchases. It does not replace ongoing monitoring. [Auto Roll](https://www.fidelity.com/fixed-income-bonds/fixed-income-tools-services/auto-roll-program)
- The nonretirement Earnings Automatic Withdrawal form permits distributions of eligible dividends, interest, and capital-gain earnings: daily, weekly, monthly, semimonthly, quarterly, annual, and custom schedules. It can send to a bank or another Fidelity nonretirement account. **Semimonthly is not biweekly.** The form distributes eligible earnings; do not assume T-bill discount maturity cash is handled identically to a coupon or that each week has earnings. [Official earnings withdrawal form, pages 1–3](https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/earnings-automatic-withdrawal-plan-nonret.pdf)
- Cash Management Account has no account fee/minimum, debit spending, Bill Pay, and transfers between Fidelity accounts. Its money-market and bank-deposit alternatives have different protections. [Cash Management](https://www.fidelity.com/investing/cash-management)

**Analysis:** Customers' difficulty finding these functions may validate an onboarding/interface problem, but does not yet validate switching custody to a new startup. A guided service on an existing broker may deserve testing alongside a new brokerage account.

## Low-complexity baseline that must be beaten

One plausible comparison is **a short-Treasury ETF plus a cash buffer and scheduled cash withdrawals**, or **a T-bill ladder plus maturity/reinvestment instructions**. This is a product-test comparator, not a recommendation or an assertion of identical risk.

- SGOV holds exposure to 0–3 month Treasury bills, charges **0.09%** expense ratio, and distributes **monthly**. An investor owns ETF shares rather than individually earmarked bonds with a personal principal repayment date. [iShares SGOV](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/314116/ishares-0-3-month-treasury-bond-etf)
- Fidelity's tools above can supply some investment and distribution components, but no verified end-to-end walkthrough was performed. Exact automation must be tested in an eligible account; the cash buffer remains necessary to promise a specified schedule that differs from asset receipts.
- A weekly **transfer** from savings can be created without corporate credit exposure. InCreditble must show what additional benefit the customer receives after fees, taxes, credit losses, execution costs, reserve drag, and effort.

## Questions for the founder — analysis and hypotheses

1. If Wealthfront already combines tax-aware bond ETFs, dividend sweeps, and a debit card, what specific outcome would make your first customer move money to you?
2. Are you selling superior net returns, a dependable spending budget, or less work? Which one is essential and how will you measure it against the alternatives?
3. If the customer has $10,000 and wants “meaningful” weekly income, will you refuse an unsuitable target—or will the interface tempt them toward credit risk to make the dollar amount look attractive?
4. At 0.25% annually, a $10,000 account produces **$25 gross revenue/year**. What can you deliver and support within that, before acquisition, custody, compliance, and banking costs? (Arithmetic, not a pricing recommendation.)
5. If your institutional advantage is relative-value swaps, where is the evidence that retail odd-lot execution and taxable turnover leave customers better off than a bond ETF or passive ladder?
6. If the goal is income certainty, why permit casual “rerolls” of portfolios or preferred-name choices that can weaken diversification and constraint discipline?
7. Would the user prefer directing proceeds to their existing bank over adopting your card? Have any interviewees asked for a new card, or is that the founder's product hypothesis?
8. Can the user explain why an annualized weekly amount is not a guaranteed weekly coupon payment, and why principal maturities are not earned income?
9. What happens when net income falls or a coupon is missed: reduce the next payout, draw the reserve, sell assets, or consume principal? Is that policy visible before funding?
10. Can you win a narrow segment with a specific use case before building portfolio management, brokerage, cash accounts, cards, and tax projections together?

## Scope limits

Public documentation verifies feature claims, not customer satisfaction, realized performance, or competitor implementation quality. No live account was opened and no trade placed. Exact yields are deliberately excluded from rankings because dates, duration, taxes, and credit exposure differ. This bounded comparison prioritizes three integrated US providers and an ETF baseline; it does not claim an exhaustive startup landscape. Jiko was not added as a direct retail competitor without confirming a comparable currently available consumer journey.
