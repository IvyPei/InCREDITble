# InCreditble: direct bond competitors and adjacent startups

Research date: **2026-10-02**. US retail scope. Official product pages, help centers and company-issued announcements only. This is desk research: no account opening, trading, execution-quality audit or verification of customer adoption. Rates are observations, not live quotes. Missing a feature from reviewed pages does not establish that the company lacks it.

## Decision-relevant findings

**“Customers choose an income/risk strategy; software selects individual bonds” is already offered at retail account sizes.** Webull is the strongest direct challenge. Public also removes much of the CUSIP-selection work and displays future income. A simpler interface is useful but insufficient evidence of a business advantage.

InCreditble's potentially distinct hypothesis is **a tax-aware spending plan with explicit cash reserves, principal-return visibility, and user-controlled payout timing**. This remains a hypothesis: after-tax arithmetic and annual income divided by 52 do not yet implement that service. Nor does institutional portfolio-management experience, by itself, establish better outcomes after retail trading costs.

## 1. Webull Managed Bond Portfolios — closest functional competitor

**Officially announced July 27, 2026.** Webull Advisors' company-issued announcement describes US-only discretionary individual-bond portfolios powered by Moment. Treasury Enhanced Cash starts at **$500 / 0.15% annual fee**; corporate High Income at **$2,000 / 0.30%**. The release's **6%+ annualized income objective** is a target, not achieved performance or guaranteed return. [Company announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/webull-launches-managed-bond-portfolios-powered-by-moment-302834505.html)

Its current help center provides opening instructions, so evidence extends beyond an announcement, although we did not open an account. Investors beneficially own individual/fractional bonds in an SMA. Portfolios generally contain 5–15 Treasuries or 20–50 corporate bonds. The algorithm selects securities; clients choose strategy rather than individual bonds. Fees have a **$1 monthly minimum**; corporate transactions can also earn Webull a spread. Yield is displayed as **YTW**, with separate net-fee time-weighted performance reporting. Coupon/maturity proceeds are reinvested above a threshold; withdrawals sell holdings and may take 3–5 trading days. The FAQ explicitly says **tax efficiency is not a portfolio-management factor**. [Managed Bond Portfolios FAQ](https://www.webull.com/help/faq/11139) (undated, checked October 2)

**Challenge:** Why would a customer transfer $10,000 to InCreditble instead of paying approximately $30/year for Webull's corporate strategy? That $30 is advisory-fee arithmetic only, excluding spreads, and assumes a constant $10,000 balance. A credible answer could involve tax-aware outcomes or payout reliability; “we pick the bonds” cannot be the entire answer.

## 2. Public Bond Account and Treasury Account — accessibility plus income visibility

The Bond Account advertises **$1,000 to buy ten fractional investment-grade/high-yield corporate bonds**, regular coupon receipts, and a twelve-month earnings view. Its observed **6.54%** headline is an average annualized **pre-fee YTW**, not a coupon rate, spendable after-tax yield or guarantee. Its expected twenty coupon payments per year are explicitly **unevenly distributed**. [Bond Account](https://public.com/bond-account) (undated, checked October 2)

The January 16, 2026 methodology describes customer-set target allocations, automated $100-par purchases toward underweights, and no allocation changes/rebalancing without instructions except corporate actions. That is not equivalent to an actively managed discretionary optimizer. Residual cash earns no interest. [Purchase methodology](https://help.public.com/en/articles/11504953-how-bonds-are-purchased-for-your-bond-account)

**Fee freshness matters:** older pages still state $3.99/month. The newer Reserve-rollout FAQ says the Bond Account fee is now free for everyone. The **September 23, 2026** schedule lists $0 account maintenance, with corporate transaction fees of **$0.35 per $100 par for 0–1-year bonds; $0.50 for longer maturities**. These are transaction charges, not annual expense ratios. [New rollout FAQ](https://help.public.com/en/articles/17044870-i-m-a-free-user-does-anything-change-for-me-with-the-reserve-rollout), [current fee schedule](https://public.com/documents/fee-schedule/)

Public also offers a **$1,000 Treasury ladder**, custom or prebuilt, with reinvest/cash-out choices; current annual advisory tiers start at 0.29%, plus transaction markups. [Treasury Account](https://public.com/invest/treasuries), [fee schedule](https://public.com/documents/fee-schedule/)

**Challenge:** Does “weekly payday” cause enough additional customer value to justify reserving cash and operating payouts, relative to an existing low-minimum bond account with a projected-income view?

## 3. Vestiva / Xendia — comparable risk-profile approach, different distribution and ticket

Single Bond Portfolios advertise conservative, moderate and growth options, questionnaire-based recommendations and automatic investment. Public/private issuers span international markets; the page says US and non-US residents. The stated product minimum is **$50,000**. [Single Bonds](https://vestiva.us/single-bonds) (undated, checked October 2)

The FAQ distinguishes the profiles using maturity and average credit quality, and permits switching profiles. It is a closer design analogue to a risk selector than a trading screen. [FAQ](https://vestiva.us/faq)

The pricing page states **1.75% on the first $100,000**, declining marginal tiers, and no separate trading/rebalancing/profile-change fees. However, it also contains a notice that opening is available only through a financial adviser; another signup widget says $250,000 minimum. Treat direct self-service availability and the conflicting minimum as **unresolved**, rather than claiming a clean $50,000 retail onboarding path. [Pricing](https://vestiva.us/prices), [product page](https://vestiva.us/single-bonds)

**Challenge:** Is InCreditble serving low-ticket beginners, or people with enough investable assets for income to change their lives? Those segments imply different acquisition channels and service economics.

## 4. Worthy — adjacent startup competing for the “simple passive income” customer

Worthy markets its own **$10 property bonds**, no investor fees, daily compounding, recurring purchases and purchase round-ups. Proceeds support real-estate development. This is a direct-issued credit product, not a customer-owned diversified portfolio of third-party Treasury/corporate bonds. The official page warns of loss, no FDIC insurance and possible processing delays up to thirty days for withdrawals above $50,000. Its text extraction has an unresolved APY placeholder, so no current rate is asserted here. [Worthy official product page](https://www.worthybonds.com/) (checked October 2)

**Challenge:** A customer may compare simple headline income without understanding legal/economic structure. Can InCreditble explain why its diversification, liquidity and risk controls matter without relying on an enticing yield headline itself?

## 5. Moment — infrastructure startup; possible supplier and source of competitive pressure

Moment is not established here as a direct-to-consumer substitute. Webull's announcement identifies it as the infrastructure behind bond trading, portfolio construction and operations. Moment's advisory-platform documentation describes portfolio optimization, execution and post-trade integration. [Webull company announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/webull-launches-managed-bond-portfolios-powered-by-moment-302834505.html), [Moment advisory-platform documentation](https://docs.moment.com/docs/multi-custodial-ria-platforms)

**Inference:** Some infrastructure behind InCreditble's vision is commercially supplied to existing distributors. This may lower the cost of launching, while also lowering competitors' imitation cost. Public evidence here does not establish pricing, partner acceptance, implementation effort, or availability to InCreditble.

**Challenge:** Which part must InCreditble build uniquely, and which should it buy? If the answer is “optimization,” what user-specific objective and measurable advantage survive when competitors buy similar portfolio infrastructure?

## Questions to carry into the founder interview

1. What would make a customer move actual money from Public or Webull, rather than merely prefer our mockup?
2. Is the core job maximizing after-tax income, making a reliable spending schedule, reducing bond-selection work, or avoiding equity drawdowns? Which one wins when they conflict?
3. How many interviewees explicitly prefer periodic spending over reinvestment—and how much investable capital do those people really have?
4. Will cash-flow smoothing use coupons plus a reserve, sell assets, or distribute principal? What happens during a default, call, illiquid market or falling-rate reinvestment?
5. What evidence would invalidate the “weekly income” proposition? Predetermine a behavioral test, such as a customer choosing between the same annual after-tax income delivered monthly versus weekly.

No market-share, retention, execution-quality or superior-performance claims are inferred from the existence of these products. This report deliberately avoids treating institutional-only bond-data vendors as retail competitors.
