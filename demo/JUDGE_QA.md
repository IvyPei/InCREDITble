# InCreditble judge questions

## Who is this for

Our initial design target is a first-time corporate-bond investor, particularly an older adult who prefers plain language and fewer choices. Age does not determine the explanation depth: the user tells us their familiarity. We still need to validate which customer segment has the strongest need and willingness to adopt.

## What makes the product different

The proposition is a complete beginner decision flow: start with a goal, see three understandable options, ask for an explanation, inspect the price and payments, and approve a small purchase. Fractional access or a chat interface alone is not the differentiation. We are testing whether the combined workflow improves comprehension and confidence. We are not claiming that competitors lack filters, explanations or small-order capabilities.

## Why would someone use two platforms

The intended reason is a clearer bond-specific experience. Account aggregation keeps existing investments visible so users do not have to move everything to try it. That is a hypothesis, not a solved acquisition problem. The next interviews should test whether the benefit justifies another account and whether an embedded experience inside an existing brokerage would work better.

## Is this wealth management or a trading platform

The current scope is corporate-bond discovery and user-approved trading. Portfolio aggregation supplies context. We are not presenting discretionary portfolio management, automatic rebalancing, a debit card or a recurring-payday service. Those belonged to an earlier exploration.

## Where is the AI

The current assistant interaction and explanation depth are implemented, but responses are generated by local deterministic logic. No LLM is connected. A future AI layer would explain verified bond terms and approved data with sources, acknowledge missing information, and remain separate from order execution. Today’s technical demonstration is the working decision flow and state changes, not live model reasoning.

## How are the top three chosen

Required filters enforce minimum yield, industry, repayment horizon and risk preference. Within them, scoring considers sample credit quality, yield fit, earlier repayment and known local positions. Unique issuers, industry variety and reduced near-duplicates make the shortlist useful to compare. If fewer than three distinct issuers qualify, we show fewer. The scoring is transparent demo logic, not a validated suitability or credit model.

## Are the price chart and default rates real

No. All market and issuer numbers are fixtures. The prototype illustrates how a user would review quotes and risk information; it cannot determine a fair market price or estimate a real issuer’s default probability. The bank benchmark is also illustrative. A live version needs a sourced, timestamped data and execution path before making those claims.

## Can someone really buy ten dollars of a corporate bond

The prototype models fractional positions from $10. That is a desired product experience, not a claim that we have arranged real fractional execution. We would need a supported broker/custody and ownership model, confirmed availability and economics, and clear handling of sales and payments before offering it to customers.

## Why does total value change after a trade

Cash and face value are tracked separately. Purchases use an invented ask and sales use an invented bid, while holdings use a fixed par reference. The spread can lower the portfolio total immediately. Cost basis and realized gains or losses update with fills. A live system would also need actual marks, accrued interest, fees, taxes and execution states.

## Why is experience separate from risk

Knowing financial terminology does not establish someone’s ability to bear loss. Familiarity changes explanation depth. The separate demo risk preference limits what the finder shows. Neither replaces a real onboarding or suitability process.

## Is this direct to consumer or business to business

The prototype demonstrates an end-user trading journey. Direct distribution versus embedding the workflow for an existing brokerage remains a commercial choice to test. We should not present both as established businesses. A partner-distributed experience may address the extra-account objection, but no such partnership is claimed.

## How would you make money

We have not validated pricing or unit economics. A partner-distributed workflow could be tested as software revenue; a direct service would need an appropriate operating model and transparent pricing. We should test willingness to pay and execution costs before claiming a scalable revenue model. No proposed fee is live in this demo.

## What evidence do you have

We have a working prototype and qualitative feedback that shaped the direction, including the need for clearer explanations and the objection to adding another platform. We are not claiming paid customers, conversion lift, assets under management or product-market fit. The next test should measure task completion, understanding of interest versus principal, comprehension of early-sale/default risk, and willingness to use the workflow.

## What is the next milestone

Observe first-time users completing the full journey without coaching. Ask them to explain the cash cost, payment dates and risks before confirmation. Then determine whether to distribute directly or through a partner, and validate the data, fractional execution and operating dependencies. Better comprehension must be demonstrated rather than assumed from a polished screen.

## Closing line

“We want your first bond decision to be one you understand, not one you simply click through.”
