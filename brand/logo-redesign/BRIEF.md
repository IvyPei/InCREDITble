# InCreditble · Logo redesign, round 3

## Brief

InCreditble is a prototype for people with savings who want to plan regular cash flow using Treasury and corporate bond portfolios. The logo should identify an approachable investing brand, without implying that payouts or capital are guaranteed.

**Audience:** people with savings seeking regular income; first encounters on a website, social post, app icon, and prototype card.

**Personality:** bold, playful, clear, capable. Avoid institutional stiffness, casino excitement, and childishness.

**Exact name:** InCreditble. **Campaign line:** Your portfolio. Your payday.

**Decision-maker:** Ivy. **Current deliverable:** three tested concepts for selection.

**Assumptions:** no established public recognition needs preserving; the previous two explorations are drafts. Violet/lime remains a possible palette, not a reason to choose a weak symbol. The wordmark remains important because the name's spelling is unusual.

## Redesign diagnosis

Round 1's C and dot were simple but generic; the image board relied on lighting effects. Round 2's connected iC had a better connection to the name, but needed a controlled vector construction and comparison with genuinely different alternatives. Preserve the warmth, strong weight, and precise name. Reconsider all geometry.

## Research and strategy

The installed skill's bundled payments/fintech library contains 37 examples. Its category summary shows frequent circles, blue, and abstract marks. These are reference-library observations, not a comprehensive or current market survey.

Studied the construction of Patreon (bar/circle economy), Framer (modular geometry), Pagekit (open counter), and Square (negative-space proportion). Reference marks are their owners' trademarks; none is used as an InCreditble asset.

Avoid: upward charts, shields, coins, generic C-plus-dot, bank columns, infinity loops, and credit-card outlines.

Word map: income -> rhythm -> repeating intervals; planned spending -> date -> folded page; InCreditble -> i / C -> connected letterform; useful money -> access -> open terminal. These are metaphors, not financial claims.

## Construction plan

- **A / In step:** a circular C with a short i stem joined at the lower-left; a separate circular tittle; one continuous filled body with a generous opening. The join must be inspected for accidental bumps.
- **B / Cadence:** two equal-weight broad hooks, one rotated 180 degrees; repeat the corner radius and preserve an open middle channel. No directional arrow or growth slope.
- **C / Daymark:** a rounded page silhouette with an i-shaped negative space; the cropped upper-right corner suggests a turned page. Keep the corner cut at 45 degrees and avoid miniature calendar details.

Symbols use a 256-unit canvas and filled geometry. Black and white first. Wordmarks are outlined from the locally installed Avenir Next Bold face with adjusted placement; font software is not redistributed. These are concept lockups, pending a final typography pass and font licensing confirmation before public rollout.

## Scope of checks

SVG structure, geometry, large rendering, 16/32/64 px samples, reversed appearance, mirror/rotation, and a reference-library shelf comparison. These checks do not establish trademark clearance or customer preference.

## Completed review

Two geometry passes were rendered and visually inspected. V2 moves A to the horizontal center and increases the dot/stem gap; B opens its middle channel from 32 to 48 units; C widens the negative-space stem, rounds the dot, and centers the silhouette. All v2 files contain vector outlines, with no live text, rasters, filters, or strokes. The SVG audit reported no failures; its 99–100 scores are structural heuristics, not design-quality or legal-clearance scores.

The overview and visual-check sheet were inspected at large scale and at 16/32/64 px, in black, reversed, mirrored, rotated, and blurred. The iC connection in A remains readable, although the tiny version will benefit from a dedicated optical cut. B retains its broad shape but may read as a generic exchange icon. C is robust at small sizes but has an information/document association. A is recommended for the clearest relationship to the name and its friendly tone. Reversed marks look slightly heavier and should be optically adjusted during the selected-direction kit pass.

The shelf comparison used the bundled Patreon, Square, Stripe, and Mastercard artwork for visual context only. No independent consumer recognition test or comprehensive similarity search was performed.

The installed logo-design skill calls for a concept-selection checkpoint before the full kit. These files are concept proposals; no final brand has been chosen. After selection, refine typography and small-size geometry, then create color variants, icons, and marketing applications.
